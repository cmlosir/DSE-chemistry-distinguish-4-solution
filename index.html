1. Task and safety

Aim

Four reagent bottles, labelled A, B, C and D, each contain one of these colourless solutions. Mix them in pairs, record your observations, and use the evidence to work out which solution is in each bottle.

    Procedure

    1. For each test, take 2 cm3 of each of two different solutions and mix them in a clean test tube.
    2. Shake the test tube gently (or stir with a glass rod) to mix.
    3. Observe carefully and record your observation straight after each test.
    4. Complete all six pairs: A+B, A+C, A+D, B+C, B+D, C+D.

    Safety reminders (for real laboratory work)

    • Wear safety glasses and protective gloves.
    • Avoid skin contact with all chemicals. Silver nitrate solution stains skin and clothes.
    • Never smell a gas directly. Any gas or product formed may be harmful.
    • Make sure the laboratory is well ventilated.
    • If you spill a chemical on your skin or clothes, wash it off at once with plenty of water and report it to your teacher.
    • Keep droppers clean. Hold the dropper just above the mouth of the test tube and do not let the tip touch anything except the reagent.
    • Dispose of waste as your teacher tells you, into the correct labelled waste bottles.

      3. Observation record

      For each test, first choose the category that matches what you saw, then write a short description in English.

      Writing observations in English
      • A white precipitate forms.
      • A greyish-yellow precipitate forms.
      • Effervescence occurs. / Colourless gas bubbles are given off.
      • No observable change.
      Write only what you see. Names of substances (e.g. “CO2 is formed”, “AgCl forms”) are inferences, not observations. Write “No observable change” rather than “no reaction”.
